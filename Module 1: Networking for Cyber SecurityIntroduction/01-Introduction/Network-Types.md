# Network Types

## Introduction

Networks are classified based on their geographical coverage, purpose, and architecture.

Understanding network types helps cybersecurity professionals identify security risks and implement appropriate controls.

---

## 1. PAN (Personal Area Network)

A Personal Area Network connects devices within a very short range, typically a few meters.

Examples:

- Bluetooth headphones
- Smartwatch
- Mobile phone
- Wireless keyboard

Advantages:

- Easy setup
- Low cost
- Low power consumption

Disadvantages:

- Limited range
- Lower data speed

---

## 2. LAN (Local Area Network)

A Local Area Network connects devices within a small area such as:

- Home
- Office
- School
- Laboratory

Examples:

- Office computers
- Printers
- Servers

Advantages:

- High speed
- Easy resource sharing
- Secure environment

Disadvantages:

- Limited geographical coverage

---

## 3. WLAN (Wireless Local Area Network)

A WLAN is a LAN that uses Wi-Fi instead of cables.

Examples:

- Home Wi-Fi
- Coffee shop Wi-Fi
- Office wireless network

Security Risks:

- Weak passwords
- Rogue access points
- Evil Twin attacks

---

## 4. MAN (Metropolitan Area Network)

A Metropolitan Area Network covers an entire city.

Examples:

- University campuses across a city
- City government networks
- Cable television networks

---

## 5. WAN (Wide Area Network)

A Wide Area Network connects multiple LANs across large geographical areas.

Examples:

- Internet
- Global enterprise networks
- Cloud providers

Security Considerations:

- VPN
- Firewalls
- Encryption
- IDS/IPS

---

## 6. SAN (Storage Area Network)

Used for high-speed storage communication.

Common in:

- Data centers
- Enterprise environments

---

## 7. CAN (Campus Area Network)

Connects multiple buildings within a campus.

Examples:

- Universities
- Hospitals
- Corporate campuses

---

## Which Networks Are Commonly Seen by SOC Analysts?

- Corporate LANs
- Wireless Networks
- VPN Connections
- Cloud Networks
- Internet Traffic

Analysts monitor these environments for suspicious activity, unauthorized access, and potential security incidents.

---

## Key Takeaways

- PAN connects personal devices.
- LAN connects devices within a building.
- WLAN is a wireless LAN.
- MAN spans a city.
- WAN spans countries or the globe.
- SAN is designed for storage.
- CAN connects multiple buildings within a campus.

---

## Interview Questions

### What is the difference between LAN and WAN?

A LAN covers a limited area such as an office or home and is typically owned by a single organization. A WAN connects multiple LANs over large distances, often using public or leased networks such as the Internet.

### Which network type is the Internet?

The Internet is the world's largest WAN.

### What are common wireless security risks?

- Weak Wi-Fi passwords
- Rogue access points
- Evil Twin attacks
- Unauthorized devices joining the network
